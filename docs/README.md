# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README is the entry point for OctoAcme’s project management process and the supporting guidance used across project work. OctoAcme follows a lightweight lifecycle that begins with initiation, moves through planning and execution, and closes with release and retrospective. Each phase is designed to align stakeholders, clarify ownership, and keep delivery focused on measurable outcomes and customer value.

## Project management process overview

OctoAcme starts new work by validating the business need and defining a simple one-pager with problem statement, goals, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and team roles. Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning by creating a prioritized backlog, setting acceptance criteria, estimating work, and identifying dependencies and milestones. During execution, the team tracks progress through standups, delivery syncs, demos, and project board updates while actively managing risks and blockers. At release, the team confirms quality gates, deploys with smoke testing and rollback planning, and communicates changes to stakeholders. After each sprint, milestone, or significant incident, the team runs a retrospective to capture lessons learned and convert them into action items.

## Key roles and responsibilities

The process depends on clear ownership across product, project, engineering, testing, and stakeholder functions. Product managers define outcomes and prioritize the backlog, project managers coordinate schedules, communication, and risk management, and developers build and validate features against agreed acceptance criteria. QA supports validation and release readiness, while stakeholders provide business context, approvals, and strategic direction. These roles are intentionally defined to improve clarity, accountability, and cross-functional alignment.

## Communication and governance

OctoAcme uses a consistent communication rhythm to keep teams aligned. This includes daily or regular standups, weekly project syncs, milestone demos, and stakeholder updates. Teams use a single source of truth for project status and maintain escalation paths for blockers and risks, moving from team triage to PM and product leadership, and to sponsor-level escalation when business impact is significant. Risk registers, issue tracking, and status updates are used to keep dependency and escalation decisions visible and timely.

## Quality assurance and continuous improvement

Quality is built throughout the workflow. OctoAcme expects small, reviewable pull requests with linkages to issues and acceptance criteria, CI validation, unit and integration testing where appropriate, and smoke testing for critical paths before release. Security checks, manual QA, and documented rollback plans are part of release readiness. After each sprint or milestone, the team reviews what went well, what needs improvement, and what actions should be tracked to improve delivery over time.
